---
title: Self-service and change requests
description: What employees can change themselves in Crow HR, and how changes to legal and bank details are approved.
---

Employees see their own record in **My profile** and their payslips in **Payslips**.

## Changed directly

These are saved straight away and audit-logged:

- preferred name
- personal email and mobile number
- address
- emergency contacts (up to 3)

## Changed through a request

Changes to these fields create a **change request** with an optional attachment:

- legal name, ID number, date of birth
- residency status and PR start date
- race and religion
- bank details

While the request is pending, the employee sees the new values marked **Awaiting approval**; everywhere else, including payroll, the current values stay in force.

Requests go through the [approvals engine](/docs/approvals/approvals/): by default **bank details go to payroll admins** and everything else to **HR admins**. The approver sees the change and the attachment and approves or rejects with a comment. An approved change applies from the approval date; changes can't be dated in the future.

Approvers act from Home or the Inbox. Nobody approves their own request, except an owner when no one else can.

## Segregation of duties

Nobody, including owners and payroll admins, edits their own compensation or bank details directly. A payroll admin who needs a raise or a new bank account gets it through someone else, which leaves an audit trail.

Bank-change requests, including the holder name and account ending, are visible only to the requester, payroll admins and owners. An HR admin needs the payroll admin role to see them.

## Light or dark

Crow HR follows your device's light or dark setting. To choose for yourself, open the account menu (your name or initials, top right) and pick **Theme: System, Light or Dark**. The choice is kept in this browser only, so a shared computer and your phone can differ, and signing out doesn't change it. The in-browser demo has the same setting.

Payslip PDFs and letters are documents for printing and filing, so they always stay black on white.
